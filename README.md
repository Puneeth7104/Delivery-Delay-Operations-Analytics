# 🚚 Delivery Delay & Operations Analytics

An end-to-end analytics project that finds **why orders arrive late** and **where the operational bottlenecks are**, with an interactive Streamlit dashboard, a delay-risk model and prioritised recommendations.

🔗 **Live dashboard:** https://delivery-delay-operations-analytics-zekgehi8rr2tkbc9qyhpze.streamlit.app/

> **Note:** All data is **synthetic** (generated by `generate_data.py` with a fixed seed). Delay patterns are planted deliberately so the analysis has real signal to discover. The pipeline works unchanged on real data with the same table layout.

---

## 1. Objective

Analyse delivery data to discover the main causes of late orders and quantify operational bottlenecks:

1. Combine **order, shipment, warehouse, carrier and delivery-event** data
2. Calculate **promised date, actual date, delay days and on-time %**
3. Segment delays by **warehouse, carrier, route, product type, day of week and season**
4. Use exploratory analysis to find **high-delay combinations and unusual spikes**
5. Build a **statistical model / decision tree** to estimate delay risk
6. Present an **operations dashboard** with KPIs, trend charts, hotspot tables and prioritised recommendations

## 2. Data model

| Table | Rows | Key columns |
|---|---|---|
| `orders.csv` | 15,000 | order_id, order_date, product_category, order_value, warehouse_id, destination_zone |
| `shipments.csv` | 15,000 | shipment_id, order_id, carrier_id, ship_date, promised_delivery_date, actual_delivery_date, distance_km, weight_kg, route |
| `warehouses.csv` | 5 | warehouse_id, warehouse_name, region, daily_capacity |
| `carriers.csv` | 4 | carrier_id, carrier_name, service_type |
| `delivery_events.csv` | ~77k | event_id, shipment_id, event_type (Packed, Dispatched, In Transit, Out for Delivery, Delivered, plus exceptions such as Weather Hold or Hub Congestion), event_time |

Tables are joined into one analysis table in `analysis.load_data()`.

**Metrics defined**
- `delay_days = actual_delivery_date - promised_delivery_date` (negative = early)
- `is_late = delay_days > 0`
- **On-time %** = share of orders with `delay_days <= 0`
- **Severely late** = delayed 3 or more days

## 4. Dashboard (5 tabs)

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| 📊 Overview | KPI cards (orders, on-time %, avg days late, severe delays), monthly on-time trend, delay distribution |
| 🔍 Segments | Late % by warehouse, carrier, route, product, weekday and season, plus warehouse×weekday and carrier×zone heatmaps |
| 🔥 Hotspots & Spikes | Worst warehouse × carrier × product combinations, statistically unusual warehouse-days (z-score), exception reasons |
| 🤖 Delay-Risk Model | Model comparison, top delay drivers, decision-tree rules and an order-level risk estimator |
| ✅ Recommendations | Auto-ranked actions by number of excess late orders |

Sidebar filters (date, warehouse, carrier, category, zone) apply across the dashboard.

## 5. Key findings (on the generated dataset)

- Overall **on-time rate is about 73%**, with about 4% of orders severely late (3+ days).
- **Remote destinations** are the biggest bottleneck, with a late rate of about 58% versus about 20% for metro areas.
- **SwiftLine** has a late rate of about 37%, versus about 20% for BlueArrow, the best carrier.
- **Festive season (Oct-Dec)** and **Fridays** show elevated delays, driven mainly by Mumbai Hub congestion.
- **Furniture and Appliances** are late about 40% of the time because of bulky handling.
- Spike detection flags the **Delhi transport-strike week (Mar)**, the **Kolkata flood week (Aug)** and the **November sale surge**.

## 6. Delay-risk model

Three models predict whether an order will be late, using a 75/25 stratified train-test split:

| Model | ROC-AUC |
|---|---|
| Gradient Boosting | ~0.88 |
| Logistic Regression | ~0.87 |
| Decision Tree (depth 4) | ~0.83 |

Logistic-regression coefficients provide interpretable drivers; the decision-tree rules give simple if-then logic for operations teams. The dashboard includes a risk estimator for an individual order.

## 10. Limitations and next steps

- Synthetic data: findings illustrate the method rather than a real business.
- `processing_days` is only known after dispatch, so the model is best read as a post-dispatch risk score. Drop it for a pure order-time prediction.
- Next steps: connect live data, add carrier cost data to quantify ₹ impact, add weather features, schedule a weekly refresh.
